## Supported Conclusions

- Merck describes exudative epidermitis as a generalized staphylococcal infection affecting young pigs.
- Merck states that lesions are caused by Staphylococcus hyicus, while other staphylococci have been sporadically implicated.
- Merck states that the disease occurs in pigs 5-60 days old, with the acute form usually affecting suckling piglets and a chronic form more common in weaned pigs.
- Merck describes early clinical signs as listlessness and reddening of skin, followed by moist greasy exudate and nonpruritic lesions.
- Merck states that diagnosis is usually presumptive based on clinical signs and that bacterial culture of lesions can be used when confirmation is necessary or when an isolate is desired.
- Merck lists differential diagnoses including sarcoptic mange, nutritional deficiency diseases such as parakeratosis, ringworm, and pityriasis rosea.
- Merck states that treatment selection should be guided by antimicrobial culture and susceptibility testing when antimicrobials are used.
