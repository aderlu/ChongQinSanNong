# Diseases of Swine 11e pages 1-200 chapter and type analysis

This page records a detailed intake of `raw/md/DISEASES OF SWINE1-200.md`. It is intended for source-first retrieval and for reinforcing disease pages that remain `NEEDS_REVIEW` without changing their review state.

## Source scope

The file contains front matter of the textbook, the table of contents for the whole book, contributors, editor notes, and the beginning of the textbook body. The body portion covered by pages 1-200 is mainly Section I, Veterinary Practice. It is crosscutting rather than disease-specific.

Usable evidence categories from this file:

- pathogen and disease evidence framing: indirect support through diagnostic reasoning, causality, and test interpretation
- diagnostic evidence: direct support for diagnostic process, test choice, sample timing, false positive and false negative boundaries
- sampling evidence: direct support for representative animal selection, antemortem and postmortem specimen selection, necropsy workflow, and submission context
- regulatory and management evidence: direct support for biosecurity, movement-risk reasoning, residue avoidance, prudent drug use, and public-health interface boundaries, but not jurisdiction-specific legal orders unless paired with jurisdictional sources
- disease chapter evidence: table-of-contents and source navigation support for locating later pathogen chapters

## Section and chapter map

### Section I Veterinary Practice

1. Herd Evaluation, page 3, source `SRC-0003`, raw headings around lines 2145-2438.

Use type: herd-level diagnostic framing, site visit structure, records, benchmarks, reporting, biosecurity, four-circle observation, diagnostic question formation, intervention prioritization, outcome monitoring, blood sampling, and oral-fluid collection.

Disease-page reinforcement: every disease page should avoid single-pig or single-sign overdiagnosis when a herd pattern is described. Case answers should capture stage, group size, morbidity, mortality, duration, spread, recent movement, medication, vaccination, feed, and environmental changes.

2. Behavior and Welfare, page 17, source `SRC-0004`, raw headings around lines 2467-2842.

Use type: welfare and behavior context, pain recognition, routine procedure boundaries, feeding and drinking behavior, lameness behavior, transport fatigue, stress syndromes, aggression, disease effects on behavior, and euthanasia boundary.

Disease-page reinforcement: behavioral signs are supportive evidence, not disease confirmation. Lameness, fatigue, aggression, anorexia, or altered behavior must be routed to system-based differential diagnosis and welfare-aware management.

3. Genetics and Health, page 42, source `SRC-0005`, raw headings around lines 3174-3268.

Use type: genetic influence on mortality, disease resistance, immune response, and sow productive lifetime.

Disease-page reinforcement: population-level susceptibility and production-stage risk should be considered. Genetic or line effects must not replace pathogen diagnosis.

4. Effect of Environment on Health, page 50, source `SRC-0006`, raw headings around lines 3366-3528.

Use type: temperature, humidity, ventilation failure, pen space, feeder space, water, noise, and stray voltage.

Disease-page reinforcement: respiratory, sudden-death, poor-growth, diarrhea, and welfare cases should include environment as a differential or risk amplifier when the scenario contains ventilation, heat, water, crowding, feeder, or transport clues.

5. Differential Diagnosis of Diseases, page 59, source `SRC-0007`, raw headings around lines 3594-3730.

Use type: system-based differential diagnosis across digestive, respiratory, integumentary, hemopoietic, nervous-locomotor, reproductive, zoonotic, vesicular, and urinary presentations.

Disease-page reinforcement: `NEEDS_REVIEW` disease pages can use this chapter as a syndrome-to-differential navigation anchor. It supports adding differential boundaries even when the disease page lacks complete disease-specific signs.

6. Diagnostic Tests, Test Performance, and Considerations for Interpretation, page 75, source `SRC-0008`, raw headings around lines 3738-4064.

Use type: bacterial culture and AST, clinical pathology, ELISA, fluorescent antibody tests, immunohistochemistry, microscopic agglutination, in situ hybridization, PCR, qPCR, multiplex PCR, sequencing, toxicology, virus isolation, virus neutralization, and diagnostic interpretation.

Disease-page reinforcement: a positive detection result must be interpreted with clinical compatibility, lesion compatibility, timing, sample quality, and test performance. PCR or serology alone should not be written as absolute causality.

7. Optimizing Sample Selection, Collection, and Submission to Optimize Diagnostic Value, page 98, source `SRC-0009`, raw headings around lines 4152-4418.

Use type: diagnostic question definition, representative animal selection, antemortem sampling, postmortem sampling, necropsy, thoracic, abdominal, reproductive, CNS, and locomotor tissue collection, and necropsy safety.

Disease-page reinforcement: sampling recommendations should be syndrome-specific: respiratory cases need respiratory tract and lung-oriented specimens; enteric cases need feces, contents, and intestinal tissue; reproductive cases need fetuses, placenta, and sow/gilt samples; neurologic cases need CNS collection under safety guidance; lameness or arthritis cases need joint and locomotor tissues; sudden death often needs postmortem evidence before disposal.

8. Collecting Evidence and Establishing Causality, page 112, source `SRC-0010`, raw headings around lines 4447-4624.

Use type: diagnostic process, variation in test results, sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic submissions, multiple-test strategies, surveillance, prevalence estimation, quantitative data, cutoff values, and intervention evaluation.

Disease-page reinforcement: detection is not equivalent to causation. Pages should preserve uncertainty and require convergence of history, clinical pattern, lesions, lab findings, epidemiology, and differential exclusion.

9. Disease Control, Prevention, and Elimination, page 123, source `SRC-0011`, raw headings around lines 4641-5209.

Use type: transmission, host-pathogen-environment ecology, occurrence patterns, persistence, vector-borne, short-cycle, long-cycle, resistant and commensal pathogens, causal inference, biosecurity, within-farm and between-farm controls, transport, live/dead pigs, feed, people, clothing, footwear, equipment, untreated food, and evidence-based biosecurity.

Disease-page reinforcement: control advice should be route-based. It should address isolation, animal movement, dead-pig handling, vehicles, feed, people, equipment, sanitation, and monitoring. High-consequence suspicions should escalate to official or veterinary procedures rather than farm-level treatment shortcuts.

10. Drug Pharmacology, Therapy, and Prophylaxis, page 158, source `SRC-0012`, raw headings around lines 5333-5579.

Use type: treatment objectives, antimicrobial classes, disease-specific drug selection logic, routes, production logistics, regulatory constraints, prophylaxis, regulation, withdrawal, resistance limitation, prudent use, treatment failure assessment, passive immunity, probiotics, bacteriophages, nutrients, antiparasitics, hormones, and anti-inflammatory drugs.

Disease-page reinforcement: treatment content should remain objective-driven and source-bounded. Do not generate executable dose, course, withdrawal period, MRL, or residue promises without a label-level or equivalent source. Antimicrobial advice requires diagnosis, stewardship, monitoring, and reassessment.

11. Anesthesia and Surgical Procedures in Swine, page 171, source `SRC-0013`, raw headings around lines 5638 onward.

Use type: procedural and analgesic boundaries, restraint and support context, sedation and anesthesia categories, catheterization, and surgical context.

Disease-page reinforcement: disease QA should not drift into anesthetic or surgical protocols unless the user explicitly asks for veterinary procedural support and the answer remains professional-context bounded.

12. Preharvest Food Safety, Zoonotic Diseases, and the Human Health Interface, page 197, source `SRC-0014`, table-of-contents anchor lines 149-150.

Use type: physical, chemical, and biologic hazards, drug residues, MRSA, feed safety, certification programs, zoonotic disease boundary, and human-health interface.

Disease-page reinforcement: zoonotic, residue, food-safety, and public-health claims require explicit source scope and, when jurisdiction-specific, official local regulatory sources.
