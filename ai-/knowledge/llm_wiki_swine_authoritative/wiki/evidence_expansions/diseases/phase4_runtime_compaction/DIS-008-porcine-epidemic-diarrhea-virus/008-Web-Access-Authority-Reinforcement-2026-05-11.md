# DIS-008 PED Web Access Authority Reinforcement

## Sources

- `A1-WOAH-PED`: Porcine epidemic diarrhoea - https://www.woah.org/en/disease/porcine-epidemic-diarrhoea/
- `A1-USDA-APHIS-PED-TECH-NOTE-2023`: Porcine Epidemic Diarrhea (PED) Technical Note - https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/ped_tech_note.pdf

## Registered Fact Anchors

- `DIS008-WEB-001-woah-nonzoonotic-coronavirus` (A1-WOAH-PED): WOAH identifies PED as a non-zoonotic viral disease of pigs caused by a coronavirus and characterised by watery diarrhoea and weight loss.
- `DIS008-WEB-002-woah-age-morbidity-mortality` (A1-WOAH-PED): WOAH states that PED affects pigs of all ages but is most severe in neonatal piglets, with morbidity and mortality reaching up to 100% and mortality decreasing as age increases.
- `DIS008-WEB-003-woah-fecal-oral-biosecurity-early-detection` (A1-WOAH-PED): WOAH states that PED is mainly transmitted by the faecal-oral route and that prevention and management control focus on strict biosecurity and early detection.
- `DIS008-WEB-004-woah-clinical-similarity-no-specific-treatment` (A1-WOAH-PED): WOAH states that PED is clinically similar to other forms of porcine gastroenteritis and that there is no specific treatment; this supports diagnostic and treatment-boundary use only.
- `DIS008-WEB-005-usda-lab-differentiation-from-tge` (A1-USDA-APHIS-PED-TECH-NOTE-2023): USDA APHIS states that PED's coronavirus agent is distinguishable from transmissible gastroenteritis of swine only by laboratory tests.
- `DIS008-WEB-006-usda-fecal-oral-no-vector-reservoir` (A1-USDA-APHIS-PED-TECH-NOTE-2023): USDA APHIS states that PED transmission is fecal-oral and that no vector or reservoir has been implicated in its spread in the technical note.

## Boundary

- Do not generate China-specific reporting, quarantine, culling, movement-control, vaccination schedule, farm-closure, or market-access conclusions from WOAH or USDA pages alone.
- Do not convert no-treatment or secondary-infection wording into executable prescriptions, antimicrobial choices, dose, route, course, withdrawal period, MRL, residue, or food-safety conclusions.
- Do not diagnose PED from watery diarrhoea alone; clinical similarity to TGE and other porcine gastroenteritis requires laboratory-supported differentiation.
